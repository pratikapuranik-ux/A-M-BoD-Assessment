# Framework and Rubric: Day 1 / Day 2 Board Assessment (board-only)

Six dimensions, 22 indicators. Dimensions and indicators are **level-neutral**: anchors describe behaviour, never the evidence. Every indicator is scored **once, for the board**. Management's observable actions are attributed to the board's oversight: a listed board is accountable for what the company does under its watch.

Indicator scores: 1 = Day 2, 3 = Mixed, 5 = Day 1 (2 and 4 only when evidence meets parts of both adjacent anchors).

Outside-in anchor overrides for 1.2 and 4.1 live in `outside_in_overrides.md`; apply them when scoring outside-in and leave the anchors below untouched for other levels.

Scoped pairs (an evidence item is primary for one indicator only): 3.4 / 5.1, 5.4 / 6.3, 1.4 / 6.1, 2.3 / 3.3.

Peer-relative indicators ('in line with peers' = 3): 3.2, 5.1, 6.1, 6.2.

Silence in a filing is scorable in full on 1.4, 5.2, 5.3; capped at 3 (Low confidence) on 2.3, 2.4, 4.3; a documented empty register search supports up to 3 on 1.3.

## Dimension 1: Customer Obsession

Whether the board's attention stays on the end customer or drifts toward internal metrics and politics.

### 1.1 Customer evidence in board and management attention

**What is being assessed:** Whether customer outcomes, including unflattering evidence, get sustained attention from the board and senior management rather than appearing only inside financial results.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Customer appears only as revenue or market share inside financial reporting and messaging. |
| 3 - Mixed | Periodic customer reviews based on management-curated metrics (e.g., NPS, retention), selectively surfaced. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Customer outcomes are a standing priority; unfiltered evidence (complaints, churn drivers, lost deals) is routinely examined and openly acknowledged. |

**Board lens:** Does the board keep customer outcomes as a standing priority, ask for unfiltered evidence (complaints, churn drivers, lost deals), and hold management to leading with customer outcomes in its reporting and external messaging rather than curated financial summaries?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Investor communications (earnings calls, shareholder letters, investor days); regulatory filings (annual report KPI disclosures); third-party data (analyst reports)

**Outside-in availability:** High

### 1.2 Unmediated customer and frontline exposure

**What is being assessed:** Whether directors and senior leaders have regular, structured contact with customers and frontline staff that management does not curate.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | No director or senior leader has had unmediated customer or frontline contact in two or more years. |
| 3 - Mixed | Occasional site visits or customer events, staged and curated by management. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Regular, structured, unmediated exposure of directors and senior leaders to customers and frontline staff, with findings brought back to the board. |

**Board lens:** Do directors have structured, unmediated contact with customers and frontline staff, and has the board required senior leaders to do the same and to bring findings back to the boardroom rather than staging visits?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (director biographies, skills matrix, disclosed engagement programmes); investor communications (management field references); press and trade media

**Outside-in availability:** Low (overridden to Low-Medium outside-in)

> Outside-in: use the override anchors in `outside_in_overrides.md`.

### 1.3 Customer-harm early warning

**What is being assessed:** How early customer-harm signals, including key customers actively spreading negative views, reach the board, and whether the board acts on them.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Customer issues reach the board through press, regulators, litigation or a key customer going public. |
| 3 - Mixed | Issues reach the board after management has resolved them. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Early-warning signals, including a watch list of key customer detractors, flow to the board routinely, and the board has acted on one in the last 12 months. |

**Board lens:** Has the board asked for early-warning signals, including a key-customer detractor watch list, required management to escalate customer harm early, and acted on a signal in the last 12 months?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Regulator, court and public records (enforcement actions, complaint databases, litigation); third-party data (review and complaint platforms, key customers' own public statements); press and trade media; investor communications (acknowledgement and remediation)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 1.4 Customer-linked accountability

**What is being assessed:** Whether executive incentives carry a meaningful customer or quality metric with real downside when it is missed.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Executive incentives are entirely financial. |
| 3 - Mixed | Customer or quality metrics included at low weight or with easily met targets. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Meaningful customer or quality metric in incentives, with demonstrated reduction in payout when missed. |

**Board lens:** Has the compensation committee put a meaningful customer or quality metric into incentives, cascaded it below the C-suite, and enforced the downside when it was missed?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (remuneration report / proxy compensation discussion, incentive scorecards and payouts)

**Outside-in availability:** High

## Dimension 2: Resisting Proxies

Whether decisions are grounded in fresh evidence of what's working, or in inherited process and precedent that's outlived its purpose.

### 2.1 Self-initiated assumption challenge

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board and management revise core strategic assumptions on their own initiative in response to new evidence, rather than under external pressure.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | No material revision of a core strategic assumption in recent years despite changing evidence. |
| 3 - Mixed | Revisions happen, but only after a miss, activist pressure or analyst challenge. |
| 5 - Day 1 | A material revision of a core assumption was initiated internally within the last 18 months, based on new evidence, ahead of external pressure. |

**Board lens:** Did the board initiate or demand revision of a core assumption on new evidence, ahead of a miss, activist or analyst, and did it require management to bring that evidence rather than wait for external pressure?

**Time window:** 36-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (strategy sections year on year); investor communications (investor days, guidance framework changes); capital markets records (activist letters, 13D filings); third-party data (analyst notes)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 2.2 Metric-versus-reality testing

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board and management test that headline metrics still measure what matters, including against frontline and external reality.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Headline KPIs accepted at face value; definitions change without explanation. |
| 3 - Mixed | Occasional deep dives into specific metrics. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Metrics are routinely tested against frontline and external reality and retired or redefined transparently when they stop measuring what matters. |

**Board lens:** Does the board test whether its pack metrics still measure what matters, press for frontline and external reality checks, and require KPIs to be redefined or retired transparently rather than defended?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (KPI definitions, non-GAAP reconciliations); regulator records (SEC comment letters); third-party data (market share, traffic and pricing trackers, short-seller and analyst critiques)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 2.3 Unfiltered internal information

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board hears from management below the C-suite and from independent advisors it appoints, rather than only through the CEO and CFO.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | All board information arrives via the CEO and CFO. |
| 3 - Mixed | Some direct access to executives below the C-suite; independent advisors limited to statutory roles. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Regular unfiltered access to management below the C-suite and to independent advisors appointed by the board. |

**Board lens:** Has the board secured regular access to management below the C-suite, appointed its own independent advisors, and prevented the CEO and CFO from gatekeeping information flows?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (board leadership structure, committee charters, governance guidelines on access to management and advisors, disclosed advisor appointments)

**Outside-in availability:** Low

> Low availability: score only where evidence is found; otherwise mark not assessable with its cause (never default to 3).

### 2.4 Process sunset

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board actively retires processes, reports and committees that no longer earn their place.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Committee structures, reporting packs and policies essentially unchanged for years. |
| 3 - Mixed | Periodic reviews with minor changes. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Processes, reports and committees are actively retired or redesigned as the strategy changes. |

**Board lens:** Does the board retire its own committees, standing reports and processes when they no longer earn their place, and require management to simplify the board pack and retire redundant forums?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (committee structure, charters and meeting counts over five years)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

## Dimension 3: Embracing External Trends

Whether the board are shaping the company's response to industry shifts or reacting after competitors have already moved.

### 3.1 Ownership of named structural shifts

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board and management systematically track two or three named structural shifts with an explicit company position on each.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Trends discussed only when management raises them or after a peer moves; shifts appear only as risk factors. |
| 3 - Mixed | Covered at the annual strategy offsite or in narrative, without an explicit position or targets. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Two or three named structural shifts tracked systematically, each with an explicit position, targets and clear oversight. |

**Board lens:** Does the board own explicit positions on two or three named structural shifts, with targets and oversight, and require management to treat them as strategy rather than risk factors?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (strategy and risk factors); investor communications (investor days); governance disclosures (oversight of strategy, technology, sustainability)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 3.2 Relative timing on sector shocks

**What is being assessed:** Whether the company moved ahead of, alongside or after peers on the most recent sector shocks.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Last mover or visibly reactive on the last two or three sector shocks. |
| 3 - Mixed | Broadly in line with peers. |
| 5 - Day 1 | First or early mover, with public commitment ahead of peers. |

**Board lens:** Did the board prompt or clear early moves on the last two or three sector shocks, and did the company under its oversight move ahead of, alongside or after peers?

**Time window:** 24-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Capital markets records (announcement timelines versus peers); investor communications; press and trade media; third-party data (analyst reports)

**Outside-in availability:** High

### 3.3 External expertise and perspective

**What is being assessed:** Whether independent experts, customers and investors inform the board routinely, and whether board skills cover the domains of the named shifts.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | No external input beyond management and auditors; board skills do not cover the named shifts. |
| 3 - Mixed | Occasional external speakers; one director with relevant expertise. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Independent experts, customers and investors brought in routinely; board skills and recent appointments explicitly cover the named shifts. |

**Board lens:** Do board skills and recent appointments cover the named shifts, and does the board bring outside experts, customers and investors into the room and into the company's planning?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (skills matrix, director biographies, appointment announcements, advisory boards); investor communications (investor day external speakers)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 3.4 Capital behind conviction

**What is being assessed:** Whether capital (capex, R&D, M&A) visibly follows the company's stated positions on the named structural shifts. Scope: direction of capital relative to stated positions; the overall build-versus-defend mix is 5.1.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Stated positions on structural shifts with no visible capital behind them. |
| 3 - Mixed | Modest, incremental allocation to the stated positions. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Capex, R&D and M&A visibly follow the stated positions, at a scale consistent with the conviction expressed. |

**Board lens:** Did the board approve capex, R&D and M&A at a scale matching the conviction expressed on the named shifts, and challenge gaps between the stated positions and actual allocation?

**Time window:** five years of filings

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (cash flow, segment capex, R&D); capital markets records (M&A announcements); investor communications (capital allocation framework)

**Outside-in availability:** High

## Dimension 4: High-Velocity Decision-Making

How quickly reversible ("two-way door") decisions are taken and delegated versus the rigor applied to irreversible ones.

### 4.1 Reversible decisions: classified, delegated, fast

**What is being assessed:** Whether reversible decisions are distinguished from irreversible ones, delegated to management, and taken at first presentation.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | All decisions go through the same process regardless of reversibility; reversible decisions take multiple meetings or are deferred for more information. |
| 3 - Mixed | Distinction made informally and inconsistently; reversible decisions take one to two cycles. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Reversible decisions explicitly distinguished and delegated or decided at first presentation; initiatives are scaled or stopped quickly without blame. |

**Board lens:** Does the board explicitly distinguish reversible from irreversible decisions, delegate the reversible ones, and hold management to scaling or stopping initiatives quickly without blame?

**Time window:** 24-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (delegation of authority, matters reserved); investor communications and press (initiative timelines: announced, scaled, stopped)

**Outside-in availability:** Low-Medium (overridden to Low-Medium outside-in)

> Outside-in: use the override anchors in `outside_in_overrides.md`.

### 4.2 Irreversible-decision rigor

**What is being assessed:** Whether major irreversible decisions (large M&A, CEO selection, material leverage) get alternatives, independent views and the willingness to defer or decline.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Major irreversible decisions approved on management's timetable with limited challenge; poor outcomes (impairments, emergency succession) follow. |
| 3 - Mixed | Standard diligence and debate. |
| 5 - Day 1 | The board slows down, demands alternatives, runs a pre-mortem or independent view, and has deferred or declined at least once. |

**Board lens:** Does the board slow down on irreversible decisions, demand real alternatives, pre-mortems and independent views, and has it deferred or declined one rather than accepting management's timetable?

**Time window:** 24-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (goodwill impairments); capital markets records (merger documents, fairness opinions, terminated deals, CEO transition announcements); third-party data (rating agency commentary)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 4.3 Candor, dissent and commitment

**What is being assessed:** Whether challenge happens openly in the full board, minority views are recorded, and the board then aligns behind decisions rather than relitigating them.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Unanimity is the norm and dissent is seen as disloyal, or decisions are relitigated after the fact. |
| 3 - Mixed | Dissent surfaces mainly in executive session or offline; occasional relitigation. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Open challenge in the full board, minority views recorded and revisited, clear decision, and the board aligns behind it. |

**Board lens:** Is there open challenge in the full board with minority views recorded, does the board align behind decisions afterwards, and does it require the same of the executive team?

**Time window:** 36-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures and capital markets records (director resignations with disclosed disagreement, proxy contests); investor communications (consistency of strategy over time); press

**Outside-in availability:** Low

> Low availability: score only where evidence is found; otherwise mark not assessable with its cause (never default to 3).

## Dimension 5: Building over Protecting

Whether the board invest in and renew for the future, or defend the present: capital posture, renewal, self-scrutiny, and the lever pulled under pressure.

### 5.1 Growth over defence capital posture

**What is being assessed:** Whether deployable capital overall goes to building the future (growth capex, R&D, growth M&A) or to protecting the present (EPS-support buybacks, defensive M&A, cost as the only lever). Scope: overall mix; alignment to named shifts is 3.4.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Shareholder returns and defensive moves dominate; capex below depreciation; growth investment squeezed to protect near-term earnings. |
| 3 - Mixed | Balanced split; capex at or near depreciation. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Majority of deployable capital goes to growth consistent with the strategy; buybacks are opportunistic rather than EPS support. |

**Board lens:** Does the board set and enforce a capital allocation posture weighted to building rather than protecting near-term earnings, and protect growth investment when earnings come under pressure?

**Time window:** five years of filings

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (cash flow: capex, D&A, buybacks, dividends, acquisitions over five years); investor communications (capital allocation framework)

**Outside-in availability:** High

### 5.2 Board and leadership renewal

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board refreshes itself and the leadership pipeline ahead of need, tied to the skills the strategy requires.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Long average tenure, no recent new directors, no succession pipeline; age or term limits absent or waived. |
| 3 - Mixed | Periodic refresh; succession planning exists but is reactive. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Regular renewal tied to the skills matrix; visible succession pipeline for board and CEO; limits applied. |

**Board lens:** Does the board refresh itself ahead of need against the skills matrix, apply its own limits, plan CEO succession early, and require a leadership pipeline below the CEO?

**Time window:** five years of filings

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (tenure, age, appointment dates, refresh and retirement policies, succession disclosure); capital markets records (appointment and departure announcements)

**Outside-in availability:** High

### 5.3 Self-scrutiny and accountability to owners

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board evaluates itself rigorously, acts on the findings, and stays open to challenge from owners rather than insulating itself.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Evaluation is a compliance exercise; protective provisions or structures insulate the board; owner feedback is ignored. |
| 3 - Mixed | Regular internal questionnaire-based evaluation; some protective provisions, but the board responds to owner votes. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Periodic externally facilitated evaluation with actions tracked to completion; the board is openly accountable to owners and demonstrably responds to their feedback. |

**Board lens:** Does the board evaluate itself rigorously, act on the findings, stay openly accountable to owners rather than insulating itself, and require management to engage owners openly?

**Time window:** most recent filing cycle plus events in the 24-month window

**Outside-in source types:** Governance disclosures (evaluation disclosure, charter and bylaw provisions, vote results, responses to shareholder proposals); third-party data (proxy advisor reports)

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

### 5.4 Response to pressure: build or retrench

**What is being assessed:** Which lever the board and management reach for first when revenue or margin comes under pressure. Scope: which lever; timing relative to stress is 6.3.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Cost cutting, portfolio retreat and guidance management are the primary response. |
| 3 - Mixed | Mixed response: cost actions alongside limited reinvestment. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Reinvestment, pricing and innovation lead the response, with cost discipline as an enabler rather than the strategy. |

**Board lens:** When pressure hit, did the board push for reinvestment alternatives rather than default to approving cuts and guidance management, and which lever did the company pull first under its oversight?

**Time window:** 24-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Investor communications (calls around guidance cuts); capital markets records (restructuring announcements); regulatory filings

**Outside-in availability:** Medium

## Dimension 6: Liquidity and Capital Management

Whether the board manage to cash and balance-sheet resilience rather than reported earnings, and act while options are still open ("liquidity is altitude").

### 6.1 Cash over earnings orientation

**What is being assessed:** Whether the board and management manage to cash generation rather than reported earnings, including in incentives and messaging. Scope: cash metrics in incentives; customer metrics are 1.4.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Attention and incentives centred on EPS or adjusted earnings; cash conversion weak and not discussed. |
| 3 - Mixed | Cash flow discussed; cash metrics carry low weight in incentives. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Free cash flow and cash conversion are central to decisions, incentives and messaging, and consistently strong relative to peers. |

**Board lens:** Does the board govern to cash: cash metrics in incentives, standing cash review, decisions tested against cash generation, and management held to managing and messaging on cash conversion?

**Time window:** five years of filings

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (cash flow versus net income over five years); governance disclosures (incentive metrics); investor communications

**Outside-in availability:** High

### 6.2 Balance sheet and cash discipline

**What is being assessed:** Whether leverage, maturities, liquidity headroom and working capital are managed to preserve options through the cycle.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Concentrated near-term maturities, tight covenant headroom, leverage above peers, deteriorating working capital. |
| 3 - Mixed | Maturity profile, leverage and working capital in line with peers. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Laddered maturities, ample committed liquidity, leverage below peer median through the cycle, improving working capital. |

**Board lens:** Has the board set and enforced a leverage and liquidity policy, required regular stress tests, and held management to laddered maturities, committed liquidity and improving working capital?

**Time window:** five years of filings

**Outside-in source types:** Regulatory filings (debt notes, MD&A liquidity, working capital); capital markets records (credit agreements, rating agency reports); third-party data (peer comparables)

**Outside-in availability:** High

### 6.3 Acting ahead of stress

**What is being assessed:** Whether financing, portfolio and cost actions are taken while options are still open rather than after a downgrade, miss or covenant pressure. Scope: timing; which lever is 5.4.

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 - Day 2 | Refinancing, divestments or cost actions taken only after visible stress. |
| 3 - Mixed | Actions timely but reactive to early stress signals. |
| 5 - Day 1 | Proactive refinancing and portfolio moves well ahead of stress, under a stated policy; options ('altitude') preserved. |

**Board lens:** Has the board set early-warning triggers for liquidity and acted while the numbers still looked fine, requiring refinancing, divestment or portfolio action ahead of visible stress?

**Time window:** 24-month lookback

**Outside-in source types:** Capital markets records (refinancing and divestment announcements versus rating actions and guidance changes); investor communications

**Outside-in availability:** Medium
