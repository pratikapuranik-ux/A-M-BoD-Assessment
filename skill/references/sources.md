# Approved Sources (outside-in)

Tiers describe how hard a document is to obtain, **not** confidence. Confidence is assigned per evidence item (see scoring rule 4).

- **Tier 1** is run for the company and every peer in every assessment.
- **Tier 2** is used whenever the content can be reached. For the prototype, no source is gated: if the publisher is reputable (a recognised proxy advisor, rating agency, sell-side house, or a data platform such as Capital IQ or AlphaSense), use the information and cite it.
- **Tier 3** is used opportunistically, where the document exists.

## The nine documents

### 1. Annual report / 10-K (with quarterly 10-Qs)  (Tier 1)

**What it is:** Audited annual (and quarterly) filing: financial statements, MD&A, strategy, risk factors, KPI definitions, debt and liquidity notes.

**How to use it:** Primary quantitative source. Read five years back to build trends in capital flows, cash conversion, leverage and working capital; compare strategy and risk-factor language year on year for assumption changes.

**Information it yields:** Capex vs D&A, buybacks, R&D, M&A spend, FCF vs net income, maturities, covenants, KPI definitions and non-GAAP reconciliations, named structural shifts, impairments.

**Indicators primarily served:** 1.1, 2.1, 2.2, 3.1, 3.4, 4.2, 5.1, 6.1, 6.2

### 2. Proxy statement (DEF 14A), with governance guidelines, committee charters, charter and bylaws  (Tier 1)

**What it is:** Annual shareholder-meeting filing covering the board itself, plus the standing governance documents it references.

**How to use it:** Primary source for everything about the board. Read five years to see renewal, committee changes and evaluation disclosure evolving; read the compensation section for incentive design and actual payouts.

**Information it yields:** Director bios, tenure, age, skills matrix, appointments and departures, committee structure and meeting counts, board leadership, access to advisors, matters reserved, incentive metrics and weights, payout outcomes, evaluation process, protective provisions, vote results and responses.

**Indicators primarily served:** 1.2, 1.4, 2.3, 2.4, 3.1, 3.3, 4.1, 5.2, 5.3, 6.1

### 3. Earnings call transcripts (with CEO shareholder letter and investor day materials)  (Tier 1)

**What it is:** Verbatim record of quarterly results calls, including analyst Q&A, plus the two set-piece communications.

**How to use it:** Primary qualitative source for what management emphasises and how it responds under challenge. Read the last four to eight quarters; weight analyst Q&A over prepared remarks; pay attention to calls around guidance cuts.

**Information it yields:** Share of discussion on customers vs financials, acknowledgement of customer problems, stated positions on structural shifts, pilots announced/scaled/stopped, first lever pulled under pressure, cash vs earnings framing, capital allocation framework.

**Indicators primarily served:** 1.1, 1.3, 2.1, 3.1, 3.2, 4.1, 5.4, 6.1, 6.3

### 4. Current-event filings: 8-Ks and press releases, merger documents, credit agreements  (Tier 1)

**What it is:** Date-stamped disclosures of material events as they happen (stock-exchange announcements outside the US).

**How to use it:** Build timelines: when the company moved relative to peers and relative to stress signals; identify decision outcomes and dissent signals.

**Information it yields:** Director resignations and any stated disagreement, shareholder vote results, M&A announcements and terminations, fairness opinions, CEO transitions, refinancing, divestments, restructuring programmes, activist 13D filings.

**Indicators primarily served:** 2.1, 3.2, 4.2, 4.3, 5.2, 5.4, 6.3

### 5. Business and trade press (approved outlet list, 24-month lookback, corroboration rule)  (Tier 1)

**What it is:** Published news coverage from major business press and relevant sector trade titles.

**How to use it:** Fills the gap since the last filing and establishes timing relative to peers and shocks. Score only when corroborated by a primary source (a filing, a transcript, a court record); a single recent story cannot move an indicator by more than one point. Feeds a non-scored 'recent developments' (last 90 days) section in the output.

**Information it yields:** Leadership changes, activist stakes, customer disputes, recalls, refinancing, guidance cuts, and other material events not yet reflected in the latest filing.

**Indicators primarily served:** 1.3, 3.2, 4.1, 5.4, 6.3 (timing/recency across most dimensions)

### 6. Proxy advisor reports (ISS, Glass Lewis)  (Tier 2)

**What it is:** Voting-recommendation reports produced ahead of each annual meeting; subscribed to by institutional investors.

**How to use it:** Independent, structured assessment of the board itself, applied with a consistent methodology across companies, giving a peer-relative view. Use for board-composition and pay-for-performance indicators specifically.

**Information it yields:** Board independence, tenure, age, overboarding, diversity, skills, attendance; pay-for-performance analysis; responsiveness to prior low votes/proposals; protective provisions; vote recommendations and rationale.

**Indicators primarily served:** 1.4, 5.2, 5.3 (and partly 2.4, 6.1)

### 7. Rating agency and sell-side analyst reports  (Tier 2)

**What it is:** Credit opinions (Moody's, S&P, Fitch) and equity research notes from sell-side analysts.

**How to use it:** The only external documents assessing financial policy and stated leverage/liquidity targets; also the main source of peer comparables and third-party critique of KPIs and strategy.

**Information it yields:** Leverage and liquidity policy assessment, rating actions and rationale, peer benchmarking, KPI/metric critique, strategy commentary.

**Indicators primarily served:** 2.2, 3.2, 6.2, 6.3

### 8. Activist letters and presentations  (Tier 3)

**What it is:** Public campaign materials from activist investors: open letters, investor presentations, 13D exhibits.

**How to use it:** When present, the most candid public assessment of a board and management available; also itself a Day 2 signal (a board that attracted an activist usually missed something).

**Information it yields:** Critiques of capital allocation, tenure and board composition, strategic drift, decision speed; activist's proposed remedies.

**Indicators primarily served:** 2.1, 4.2, 5.1, 5.2

### 9. Regulator, court and public records  (Tier 3)

**What it is:** Regulator enforcement and complaint databases, litigation dockets and court opinions (incl. Delaware Chancery), bankruptcy examiner reports, legislative inquiry reports.

**How to use it:** Strongest evidence when present, including rare involuntary disclosure of actual board process (minutes quoted in litigation); sporadic by nature, so used opportunistically.

**Information it yields:** Enforcement actions, customer-harm records, litigation and court findings on board oversight, examiner reconstructions of board decision-making.

**Indicators primarily served:** 1.3, 2.2 (board-process indicators where litigated)

## Approved news list (direct evidence, no corroboration required)

Bloomberg, Reuters, The Wall Street Journal, Financial Times, Forbes, AP, The New York Times, Barron's, The Economist, CNBC, Nikkei, Handelsblatt, Les Echos, The Economic Times, Mint. The Economic Times and Mint apply to Indian companies.

Recognised sector trade press (e.g. Food Business News, FoodNavigator, Supermarket News) is direct evidence for event facts (what was announced, when, at what value) and context only for interpretation or opinion. Content accessed through S&P Capital IQ, AlphaSense or PitchBook is treated according to its underlying source. Any other outlet is context unless the event is corroborated by a direct source.

## Also approved (v1.1)

- Sector conference presentations (e.g. CAGNY for consumer staples).
- Company sustainability and integrated reports (customer metrics, stakeholder disclosure).
- Published market-share data from recognised panels (Circana, NielsenIQ) when cited through the company, a filing or approved press.
- Sector regulator registers (e.g. FDA recalls, CFPB complaints); an empty search is itself a searched-record evidence item (Rule 5).

## Minimum retrieval set before any indicator is marked not assessable (Rule 15)

1. Latest 10-K / annual report, plus five years of cash-flow statements.
2. Five years of proxy statements, read in full, including the pay discussion and governance guidelines.
3. Four to eight earnings call transcripts with analyst Q&A (CapIQ or AlphaSense preferred), including any call held on the day of a major transaction announcement.
4. A 24-month sweep of 8-Ks / exchange announcements, and a 36-month sweep for Item 5.02.
5. A merger proxy or registration statement for any pending transaction.
6. Sector regulator registers relevant to indicator 1.3 (e.g. FDA recalls for food, CFPB complaints for consumer finance).
7. Investor day materials and sector conference presentations (e.g. CAGNY for consumer staples).
8. Where accessible: ISS / Glass Lewis reports and Moody's / S&P / Fitch reports.

## Retrieval rules

- Retrieve from regulators and exchanges (SEC EDGAR and local equivalents such as SEBI/NSE/BSE, Companies House), the company's own investor-relations site, the approved news list, recognised trade press and reputable third-party publishers. Reputation of the publisher is the test, not a licence.
- Use web search to locate the document, then fetch the primary page. Cite the URL (or platform, document ID and date for licensed sources), the document title and its date for every evidence item, and tag its evidence_class.
- Never use: social media posts, anonymous forums, unverified blogs, employee review sites, AI-generated summaries without a primary citation.
- If a Tier 1 document cannot be retrieved, record it under coverage.documents_unavailable and mark affected cells retrieval-limited; do not substitute a secondary summary of it.
